Improving Supplier Discovery & Engagement in a Content-Led Platform

One Fab Day is a leading wedding inspiration platform with a curated, invite-only supplier directory. I led the UX and product design work across the platform.

The aim of this project was to improve supplier discovery, increase meaningful engagement with supplier profile pages and strengthen the value offered to paying partners.

Role

Lead UX & Product Designer

Collaboration

Editorial, AM/Sales, external engineers

Scope

Discovery, profiles, navigation, editorial integrations

Timeframe

Multi-year, iterative

Context & problem

Editorial content attracted high-intent traffic, but very little of that intent translated into supplier discovery or engagement.

Context

  • Editorial content drove strong organic traffic and engagement
  • The invite-only supplier directory was a core commercial product
  • These two parts of the site operated largely independently

Problem

  • Users weren’t moving from editorial into supplier discovery
  • Supplier pages were difficult to find and hard to scan
  • Calls-to-action were unclear and attribution was poor
  • Suppliers perceived low value due to limited visible engagement
Why it mattered: High-intent couples struggled to discover trusted vendors, while supplier confidence, renewals, and revenue were at risk.

Key insights

User behaviour

  • Couples browse inspiration for long periods, but book suppliers in short bursts
  • Supplier choice is primarily visual before becoming detail-driven
  • Users preferred visiting supplier websites over using enquiry forms

Product gaps

  • Navigation did not clearly promote supplier discovery
  • Editorial content offered few natural paths to recommended suppliers
  • Supplier pages lacked consistent trust signals and presentation
  • Attribution issues undermined supplier confidence

Goal

Connect high-intent editorial readers with curated suppliers, increasing meaningful engagement and clear, attributable actions on supplier pages.

Solution phase 1 — Improve supplier visibility at moments of intent

Editorial attracted high-intent traffic, but there were very few natural pathways into the curated supplier directory. Discovery relied on users proactively seeking suppliers, rather than encountering them in relevant moments of inspiration.

Decision 1 — Reframe the directory as a curated product

Why: Supplier quality and curation were central to the business model, but the directory was perceived as a generic listings area.

  • Renamed the directory to “The Wedding Book” to reinforce quality and curation
  • Introduced clearer category structures and surfaced them in primary navigation

Impact: Stronger trust signals and a clearer mental model of the directory as a destination.

Decision 2 — Meet users where inspiration already existed

Why: Users were highly engaged in editorial content, but had no contextual prompts to explore relevant suppliers.

  • Introduced image-based, contextually placed supplier links within editorial articles
  • Highlighted recommended suppliers within Real Wedding features

Impact: Reduced friction between inspiration and research, increasing discovery without disrupting editorial flow.

Decision 3 — Align commercial formats with editorial tone

Why: Supplier promotion needed to deliver value without undermining editorial credibility.

  • Launched Sponsor Spotlight as a dedicated editorial format
  • Maintained the same visual and narrative standards as organic content

Impact: Clearer commercial signalling and improved confidence in paid placements.

Solution phase 2 — Make supplier discovery visual and scannable

Once users entered the directory, the experience didn’t reflect how couples actually choose suppliers. Text-heavy lists made discovery slow and hard to scan in a highly visual decision-making context.

Decision 1 — Shift discovery from text-first to image-first

Why: Supplier choice is driven primarily by visual preference and emotional response.

  • Replaced text-heavy lists with image-led category pages
  • Used editorial-style imagery drawn from real wedding work

Impact: Faster scanning, stronger emotional connection, and clearer orientation within the directory.

Decision 2 — Introduce consistent supplier cards

Why: Inconsistent layouts made comparison and shortlisting unnecessarily difficult.

  • Standardised supplier cards across all categories
  • Balanced imagery, naming, and metadata to support quick comparison
  • Ensured cards worked consistently across screen sizes

Impact: Easier comparison, reduced cognitive load, and more confident shortlisting.

Decision 3 — Use hierarchy to guide attention

Why: Visual richness without hierarchy can quickly become overwhelming.

  • Established clear hierarchy using scale, spacing, and grouping
  • Prioritised scanning and exploration over completeness

Impact: Calmer pages and more purposeful browsing behaviour.

Solution phase 3 — Strengthen supplier profiles and reduce friction

Once users discovered a supplier, profile pages needed to build trust quickly and guide users toward clear, high-value actions without unnecessary friction.

Decision 1 — Prioritise visual proof

Why: Trust was established primarily through real work and visual evidence.

  • Expanded and prioritised curated image galleries
  • Ensured galleries reflected real weddings and editorial standards

Impact: Faster trust-building and increased confidence in supplier quality.

Decision 2 — Make recommendation and credibility explicit

Why: Users needed reassurance that suppliers were genuinely vetted and endorsed.

  • Added vetted testimonials from real couples
  • Introduced “Recommended by One Fab Day” indicators

Impact: Clearer credibility signals at key decision points.

Decision 3 — Reduce friction and clarify the primary action

Why: Multiple actions diluted focus and obscured partner value.

  • Removed the low-use enquiry form
  • Elevated Visit Website as the primary call to action
  • Simplified layouts to focus attention

Impact: Clearer, higher-value actions and improved attribution.

Decision 4 — Standardise layouts for scale

Why: Consistency supports faster scanning and easier comparison.

  • Standardised profile layouts across the directory
  • Improved discoverability between related suppliers

Impact: More predictable navigation and stronger perceived quality.

Results

Improving discovery, evaluation, and trust across the directory resulted in clearer user journeys and stronger outcomes for both couples and suppliers.

Supplier discovery & engagement

  • Increased discovery of suppliers from editorial content
  • Higher engagement with image-led categories and galleries
  • Clearer pathways into supplier profiles

Partner value

  • Noticeable uplift in supplier website click-throughs
  • Improved supplier satisfaction and renewal rates

Next steps

  • Explore personalised supplier recommendations based on browsing behaviour
  • Refine filtering and comparison tools to support shortlisting
  • Strengthen attribution tracking as tooling allows
  • A/B test gallery formats and call-to-action placement
